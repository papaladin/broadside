Captain’s Handbook

A quick reference for Broadside

Gold

  • Earned through missions, plunder, and trade.
  • Spent on repairs, crew, provisions, equipment, ships.
  • Wages paid daily : 2g per crew member (×1.5 if morale < 30).
  • If you run out of gold, you cannot hire, repair, or buy provisions.

Fame

  • Your permanent reputation across the Caribbean.
  • Gained from completing missions (especially combat).
  • Gates access to better ships, equipment, and hidden ports.
  • Never decays.

Infamy

  • Your criminal notoriety.
  • Gained from smuggling, attacking patrols, and certain missions.
  • At 50 infamy, bribes are blocked.
  • At 100, you become a Legendary Outlaw.

Faction Heat

  • Short‑term alert level for each colonial power (0‑10).
  • Rises when you fight their patrols or ships.
  • Decays by 1 every 2 days at sea.
  • Higher heat → more random patrols at sea.

Hull

  • Your ship’s hit points.
  • Damaged in combat and storms.
  • If it reaches 0, you are defeated.
  • Repair at any port’s Shipyard.
  • Equipment slots vary by ship: check the Shipyard to see your vessel's capacity.

Equipment & Shipyard

  • Buy, install, or remove equipment at any Shipyard.
  • Four slot types: Hull, Armament, Rigging, and Special. Each ship has a fixed number of each.
  • Higher-tier ships have more slots (e.g., Ship of the Line has 2 Armament slots).
  • Removable equipment can be uninstalled to your locker; structural equipment is permanent.
  • Buying a new ship clears all installed equipment — uninstall removable items first, or lose them.
  • View your locker and installed items in the Shipyard's Equipment tab.

Crew

  • More crew → faster combat actions.
  • Hire at ports with a Crew service.
  • Dismiss or lose crew in battle.
  • Wages paid daily; low morale can double wages.
  • Crew react to your choices – attacking their home faction can make them upset, and upset crew may desert.
  • You need a minimum crew to sail any ship larger than a dinghy (10% of the ship’s max crew).

Morale

  • Affects combat effectiveness, travel speed, and wages.
  • Drops with empty provisions, unpaid wages, or combat losses.
  • Rises with mission success, drinks, and shore leave.
  • Below 50 → travel slows. Below 30 → wages increase. At 0 → crew deserts.

Reputation

  • Per‑port standing (0‑100), shown as a label (At War → Allied).
  • At War (0–9) blocks faction services. Hostile (10–29) blocks missions. Friendly (50–79) unlocks faction services. Allied (80–100) unlocks the highest level of access.
  • Affects repair cost and mission rewards.
  • Decays slowly toward 50 over time.
  • Attacking a faction’s ships reduces reputation with all its ports.

Faction Services

  • English — Naval Yard: at 50+ reputation, service removable equipment and manage your locker. At 80+, gain early access to ships and equipment with reduced Fame requirements.
  • Spanish — Inquisitor: at 50+ reputation, pay to reduce Infamy by 1. Available once per port visit.
  • French — Embassy: at 50+ reputation, pay to increase another faction’s reputation by 5.
  • Dutch — Bank: at 30+ reputation, take a loan if you have no outstanding debt. Positive trade income is used to repay 20% of the remaining debt.
  • Pirate — Black Market: pirate ports are the exclusive source of Smuggling missions.

Speed & Hold

  • Speed determines how many days a voyage takes.
  • Hold capacity limits how much cargo you can carry.
  • Hold load > 50% slows your ship (+11% travel time).
  • Hold load > 75% slows it further (+33%).
  • Speed also matters in combat – it affects your chances to evade, flee, and to close or open distance.

Resources & Trade

  • Food & Water consumed daily (1 unit per 10 crew).
  • Empty provisions → morale loss; after 14 days no food / 3 days no water, crew die.
  • Prices vary per port based on availability and random variance.
  • Buy low in one port, sell high in another for profit.
  • Check the Good Deals and In Demand tags on the market screen — they highlight profitable routes.
  • Hovering a port on the map shows its trade profile (goods it deals in and goods it lacks).
  • Goods marked Illegal (tobacco, slaves) risk confiscation by patrols.

Intercept

  • Before every battle, you can choose: Fight, Flee, Parley, Bribe, or Surrender.
  • Navy patrols also offer Allow Inspection – if you're carrying contraband, it will be seized and you'll be fined.
  • Surrendering costs gold, cargo, days, or reputation depending on context.
  • Fleeing uses your ship’s speed (higher speed = better chance to escape).
  • Even after you've started a battle, you can Evade to try to escape (again, speed matters).
  • Random patrols (not just mission patrols) can also catch contraband when inspecting.

Combat (Naval)

  • Combat is turn‑based with three distance bands: Far, Medium, Close.
  • Broadside – reliable cannon volley (100% hit).
  • Precision – risky, high hull damage (70% hit).
  • Close Distance – move closer (Far → Medium → Close).
  • Open Distance – move farther (Close → Medium → Far).
  • Grapple – requires Close range; leads to boarding.
  • Evade – attempt to flee; only possible at Far range.
  • Speed matters – your ship’s speed affects your chances when evading, fleeing, and in contests to close/open distance.
  • Damage multipliers vary by distance (e.g., broadside is stronger at Medium; precision is stronger at Far).
  • If the enemy hull reaches 0, it is sunk – no plunder. If its crew reaches 0, it is captured – plunder available.
  • You cannot plunder a sunk ship.

Boarding

  • After a successful grapple, combat becomes a boarding fight.
  • Your advantage is shown as a split bar: crew × morale effectiveness vs. enemy's.
  • Actions: Continue Fighting (press the attack), Fall Back (return to naval, costs crew), Demand Surrender (requires 65%+ advantage), or Surrender (yield).
  • Wiping out the enemy crew leads to victory and plunder (if you choose to take it).
  • Fall Back costs you some crew – it's not a free escape.

Plunder

  • After boarding victory, you can take the enemy's cargo (limited by your hold space).
  • You automatically receive a gold reward (20% of the plunder value).
  • You can browse and choose which cargo to keep, or jettison items from your own hold to make room.
  • Illegal goods (tobacco, slaves) may be present – taking them risks patrol inspection later.

Mission Types

  • Trade – buy goods cheap, deliver for profit.
  • Smuggle – deliver illegal goods; risk patrol inspection.
  • Combat – hunt down a specific enemy ship.
  • Patrol – clear a rival’s waters of hostile vessels.
  • Escort – protect a convoy to its destination.
  • Assault – attack a port’s garrison by force.
  • Abandoning a mission – you can abandon, but you lose reputation with the commissioning faction.

Crew Traits

  • Hidden traits (drunkard, coward, greedy) – revealed over time.
  • Scars earned from storms, battles, shipwrecks.
  • Progression: Seasoned (50 days) → Veteran (100 days) → Loyal (200 days).
  • Upset crew may desert if you attack their home faction.

Navigation & Hidden Ports

  • Click a port on the World Map to see details and set sail.
  • While sailing, you can Change Course to a different reachable port.
  • Some ports are hidden – they only appear after you meet their unlock conditions (fame, infamy, or a map fragment).
  • Hidden port hints sometimes appear in port gossip.

Save & Load

  • Game saves automatically on port entry (if enabled).
  • Use the Menu (on the Port screen) to Save, Load, Export (as a file), or Import a save.
  • Export saves can be kept as backups or moved to another device.
